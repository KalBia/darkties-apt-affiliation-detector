# Literature Comparison Table --- DarkTies (PBAD)

Selected papers and reports relevant to the DarkTies project, aligned one-to-one with the bibliography of the Design Proposal (22 entries). The entries are grouped by the sections of the proposal's literature analysis: reviews and systematizations (Section 2.1), building blocks for the proposed system (Section 2.2), machine-learning and agentic elements (Section 2.3), and the APT gap (Section 2.4). Works cited elsewhere in the proposal --- the username-linkage methodology and the research-ethics source --- are grouped under method foundations and ethics.

Columns:

- **PAPER** --- authors and title.
- **LINK** --- publisher or open-access record.
- **SUMMARY** --- plain-language summary of the work.
- **USEFULNESS** --- relevance to the project (high / moderate / low), with justification.
- **CREDIBILITY** --- venue reputation and peer-review status (high / moderate / low).

| Dalyapraz Manatova, Charles DeVries, Sagar Samtani, **"Understand your shady neighborhood: An approach for detecting and investigating hacker communities"** (2024) | https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167923624001040 | The paper tests different methods for finding communities inside hacker forums. Neighborhood-based methods (like Expansion) work best, and the authors propose a step-by-step workflow for investigating hacker communities and their collaborations. | **high** (best journal paper on finding hacker groups, guides tool design) | **high** (Elsevier) |
| Baidyanath Biswas, Arunabha Mukhopadhyay, Sudip Bhattacharjee, Ajay Kumar, Dursun Delen, **"A text-mining based cyber-risk assessment and mitigation framework for critical analysis of online hacker forums"** (2021) | https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167923621001615 | The paper sorts forum users into skill levels (newbie to advanced) based on what they write and how they behave. Cybersecurity keywords, file attachments and sentiment separate experts from beginners, and expert hackers act like leaders in their communities. The paper then estimates the financial damage each skill level could cause per attack type and suggests what to fix first. | **high** (links hacker profiles to money and risk, the strongest decision-support paper in the corpus) | **high** (Elsevier) |
| Huang C, Guo Y, Guo W, Li Y. **"HackerRank: Identifying key hackers in underground forums"** (2021) | https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/15501477211015145 | The paper combines two things to find key hackers: what they post about (content analysis) and how they interact with others (a PageRank-like score over the forum network). The combined method works better than either approach alone on five underground forums. | **high** (a ready method for finding the most important actors in a forum) | **high** (International Journal) |
| Sagar Samtani, Weifeng Li, Victor Benjamin, and Hsinchun Chen, **"Informing Cyber Threat Intelligence through Dark Web Situational Awareness: The AZsecure Hacker Assets Portal"** (2021) | https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3450972 | The paper describes a working academic CTI platform that collects and analyzes dark web forums and marketplaces. It profiles hackers and their assets, reports their intentions and motivations, and helps find key authors in the forum network. | **high** (one of the few academic tools actually deployed, good architecture reference) | **high** (peer-reviewed Journal) |
| Pastrana, Sergio and Thomas, Daniel R. and Hutchings, Alice and Grace, Martin, **"Characterizing Eve: Analysing Cybercrime Actors in a Large Underground Forum"** (2018) | https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~sp849/files/RAID_2018.pdf | The paper is the large-scale behavioral characterization of forum actors that defines the genre: it studies several years of Hack Forums data, quantifies how much of the activity is cybercrime-related, and shows that the population is heterogeneous, with different types of actors (for example buyers, sellers, and technical specialists) behaving differently. The data behind it became CrimeBB, which the Cambridge Cybercrime Centre distributes to researchers. | **high** (defines forum actor analysis as a research line; the dataset is the main evaluation corpus of the project) | **moderate** (proceedings RAID 2018) |
| Ruellan, Estelle and Labrèche, François and Paquet-Clouston, Masarah, **"Identifying Key Expert Actors in Cybercrime Forums Based on their Technical Expertise"** (2024) | https://doi.org/10.1109/eCrime66200.2024.00019 | The paper identifies key expert actors across cybercrime forums by measuring technical expertise: it links forum actors to the attack patterns they discuss using CVE and CAPEC classifications, builds a bimodal actor-attack network, and applies community detection and clustering. It finds groups of actors interested in similar attack patterns across forums, that key actors are only about 4 percent of the population, and that about half of the population are amateurs with little technical expertise. Peer-reviewed at IEEE eCrime 2024; an open arXiv version is available (arXiv:2506.01848). | **high** (2024 continuation of the actor-characterization line started by Pastrana et al.; expertise measurement is a direct building block for the role-inference layer) | **moderate** (peer-reviewed proceedings, IEEE eCrime 2024, pp. 175--186) |
| Paladini, Tommaso and Ferro, Lara and Polino, Mario and Zanero, Stefano and Carminati, Michele, **"You Might Have Known It Earlier: Analyzing the Role of Underground Forums in Threat Intelligence"** (2024) | https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3678890.3678930 | The paper looks at 88 million posts from hacker forums and compares them with about 75,000 security articles and reports from the last 20 years. The authors use BERT language models (DarkBERT works best) to find mentions of threat actors, malware and vulnerabilities. Over 60 percent of threat keywords showed up in the forums before they appeared in security reports, so forums work as an early warning. | **high** (biggest study of its kind, shows forums warn about threats early, method can be repeated) | **moderate** (proceedings RAID '24) |
| Y. Ech-Chammakhy, A. Motii, A. Rabii and J. Chbili, **"EventHunter: Dynamic Clustering and Ranking of Security Events from Hacker Forum Discussions"** (2025) | https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/11352398 | The paper presents a tool that automatically finds, groups and ranks security events discussed in hacker forum posts. It groups similar posts into event clusters (for example a zero-day leak or a malware release) without using a fixed keyword list, and it ranks the events every day by how new they are, how trusted the source is, how complete the information is and how relevant it is. Tests on real forum data show it cuts the noise and highlights the most important events. | **high** (turns messy forum posts into a clean list of ranked events, helps analysts focus) | **high** (IEEEXplore) |
